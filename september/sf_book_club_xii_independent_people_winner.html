No-spoiler description

In the harsh countryside of early twentieth-century Iceland, a sheep farmer pursues one goal above all others: to live without owing anything to anyone. After years of working for someone else, he finally has land of his own and intends to protect that independence at almost any cost. What begins as a story of stubborn self-reliance gradually opens into a much larger portrait of family, poverty, dignity, isolation, social change, and the uneasy line between freedom and dependence. Laxness mixes bleakness with dry humor and the scale of an old Icelandic saga.

A few non-spoiler facts

  • Original title: Sjálfstætt fólk (“Independent People”).
  • First publication: Published in Iceland in two volumes in 1934 and 1935.
  • English translation: The classic English translation is by J. A. Thompson; an English edition appeared in 1946.
  • Author: Halldór Laxness was born in Reykjavík in 1902 and became one of Iceland’s most important twentieth-century writers.
  • Nobel Prize: Laxness received the 1955 Nobel Prize in Literature “for his vivid epic power which has renewed the great narrative art of Iceland.”
  • Genre: Literary fiction, classics, historical fiction, and social realism, with strong connections to Icelandic saga tradition.
  • Setting: Rural Iceland in the early twentieth century, amid enormous economic and social change.
  • Current U.S. paperback: Vintage’s edition is 512 pages and uses ISBN 9780679767923.
